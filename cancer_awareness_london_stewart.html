Understanding
Cancer.

Cancer is a group of diseases in which abnormal cells grow uncontrollably. Learning about cancer, its risks, treatments, and effects can help people make informed decisions and support those affected.

Explore Cancer Types Trusted Resources
01 — The basics

What is cancer?

A disease of abnormal cell growth

Cancer develops when genetic changes disrupt the normal controls that tell cells when to grow, divide, or die. These abnormal cells can form a mass called a tumor. Some cancers, such as leukemia, generally do not form solid tumors.

There isn't just one cancer

There are many different types and subtypes of cancer. They can behave differently, spread differently, and respond differently to treatment. A diagnosis therefore needs individualized medical evaluation.

02 — Know the types

Major cancer types

This is a simplified educational overview, not a complete list. Each category contains many subtypes.

BREAST

Breast Cancer

Begins in breast tissue. Risk can be influenced by age, inherited gene changes, reproductive history, hormones, and other factors.

LUNG

Lung Cancer

Starts in the lungs. Tobacco exposure is a major risk factor, although people who never smoke can also develop lung cancer.

PROSTATE

Prostate Cancer

Develops in the prostate gland. Risk increases with age and can also be affected by family history and inherited genetic factors.

COLORECTAL

Colorectal Cancer

Begins in the colon or rectum. Age, family history, certain inherited conditions, lifestyle factors, and inflammatory bowel disease can affect risk.

SKIN

Skin Cancer

Includes basal cell carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, and melanoma. Ultraviolet radiation is an important preventable risk factor.

BLOOD

Leukemia

A group of cancers involving blood-forming tissues, including bone marrow. Different leukemias can progress quickly or slowly and require different treatments.

LYMPHATIC

Lymphoma

Cancer of the lymphatic system, mainly divided into Hodgkin lymphoma and non-Hodgkin lymphoma, with many subtypes.

PANCREAS

Pancreatic Cancer

Begins in the pancreas. It can be difficult to detect early, making awareness of symptoms and risk factors important.

BRAIN & NERVOUS SYSTEM

Brain & CNS Cancers

Can begin in the brain or other parts of the central nervous system. Symptoms and treatment depend strongly on the tumor's location and type.

LIVER

Liver Cancer

Primary liver cancers include hepatocellular carcinoma. Chronic liver disease and certain infections can increase risk.

KIDNEY

Kidney Cancer

Kidney cancers include renal cell carcinoma. Smoking, obesity, high blood pressure, and some inherited conditions can affect risk.

OVARY

Ovarian Cancer

A group of cancers that begin in or around the ovaries. Family history and inherited gene changes can influence risk.

03 — Risk & prevention

What can increase risk?

Common risk factors

  • Tobacco use and secondhand smoke
  • Excess exposure to ultraviolet radiation
  • Some infections, including certain viruses and bacteria
  • Alcohol use
  • Overweight or obesity and physical inactivity
  • Increasing age
  • Family history and inherited genetic changes
  • Certain workplace or environmental exposures

Risk isn't destiny

Having a risk factor does not mean someone will develop cancer, and some people develop cancer without an obvious risk factor. Some risks can be reduced, while others—such as age or inherited genetics—cannot be changed.

Prevention can include:

  • Not smoking and avoiding tobacco
  • Protecting skin from excessive UV exposure
  • Maintaining healthy activity and nutrition habits
  • Following recommended screening guidelines
  • Getting appropriate vaccines and medical care
04 — Treatment

How is cancer treated?

Treatment depends on the cancer type, stage, location, genetic features, overall health, and personal goals.

Surgery

Removes a tumor or cancerous tissue when surgery is appropriate. It can sometimes be combined with other treatments.

Chemotherapy

Uses medicines that kill cancer cells or stop them from growing. Because these medicines can affect healthy fast-growing cells, side effects are possible.

Radiation Therapy

Uses high-energy radiation to damage cancer cells. Effects can depend on the body area treated and the radiation dose.

Immunotherapy

Helps the immune system recognize or attack cancer. Immune-related side effects can occur because the immune system may affect healthy tissues.

Targeted Therapy

Targets specific features that help cancer cells grow or survive. Testing may be used to determine whether a cancer has a targetable change.

Hormone Therapy

Blocks or lowers hormones that some cancers use to grow, particularly certain breast and prostate cancers.

Stem Cell Transplant

May be used for selected blood cancers and other conditions, often after intensive treatment that affects blood-forming cells.

Clinical Trials

Research studies test new approaches or combinations of treatments. Participation has potential benefits and risks and should be discussed with a care team.

Palliative & Supportive Care

Can help manage symptoms, treatment side effects, and emotional or practical concerns at any stage of serious illness.

Important: Cancer treatments can cause serious side effects, but the risks vary widely. Never start, stop, or change cancer treatment based only on information from a website. A qualified healthcare professional should guide treatment decisions.
05 — The human impact

More than a diagnosis

Effects on patients

Cancer and its treatment can affect energy, work or school, physical abilities, appearance, relationships, mental well-being, finances, and everyday routines. Side effects can be temporary or long-lasting, depending on the treatment and person.

Effects on families

Families and caregivers may experience fear, stress, changes in responsibilities, transportation needs, financial pressure, and uncertainty. Support can include practical help, honest communication, counseling, support groups, and respite for caregivers.

07 — Questions

Frequently asked questions

Can cancer be prevented?

Not every cancer can be prevented, but some cancers have preventable risk factors. Avoiding tobacco, protecting skin from excessive UV exposure, maintaining healthy habits, vaccination where recommended, and appropriate screening can reduce risk for some cancers.

Does having cancer always mean a person will die?

No. Cancer outcomes vary widely. Some cancers can be cured, some can be controlled for long periods, and others may be more difficult to treat. Prognosis depends on factors such as cancer type, stage, biology, and response to treatment.

Are cancer treatments dangerous?

They can have serious side effects, but treatments are prescribed because their potential benefits can outweigh their risks for a particular patient. Doctors monitor patients and may adjust treatment or provide medicines to manage side effects.

What should someone do if they are worried about a symptom?

Talk with a qualified healthcare professional. Many symptoms have causes other than cancer, but persistent or concerning symptoms deserve appropriate evaluation.
